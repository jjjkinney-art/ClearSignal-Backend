STAGE 7A  ·  HOW TO SCORE  ·  (Share this with both evaluators)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

You are evaluating responses to 10 investment questions from
three anonymous systems (A, B, C).  Your job: score each
response 1–5 on five criteria.  Total time: ~2.5–3 hours.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SETUP  (do this before opening any response file)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
1. Open STAGE_7A_SCORE_SHEET.txt — this is your score sheet.
2. Read the five criterion definitions below.  All five.  Now.
3. Keep two windows open: one for reading files, one for your
   score sheet.  Do not mix them up.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
THE FIVE CRITERIA  (each scored 1–5)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

(1) DIRECT ANSWER  ·  DA
    Does the first sentence directly address what the question
    asks?  Not "does the response eventually answer it" —
    does the OPENING LINE answer it.

    5 → First sentence IS the answer: a number, a mechanism,
        a ranking, or a verdict.  Nothing before the payoff.
    4 → Answer arrives in sentence 1–2 with minimal framing
    3 → Answer is present but buried after an intro paragraph
    2 → Question is mentioned; answer is vague or indirect
    1 → Opens with a company overview; question avoided entirely

    ⚠ Common mistake: rewarding a long response with a high DA
    score because it "eventually" answers the question well.
    If sentence 1 is "Company X is a leading semiconductor
    manufacturer…" that is a 1 on DA, regardless of what follows.

(2) SPECIFICITY  ·  SP
    Are the claims company-specific or could they appear in any
    analyst report about any company in this sector?

    5 → Every claim names a specific metric, customer, multiple,
        or comparable.  Zero generic analyst-speak.
    4 → Mostly specific; one or two hedged generalities
    3 → Mix of specific facts and generic statements
    2 → Mostly generic; occasional company name dropped in
    1 → Pure generic: "significant risk," "strong position,"
        "well-positioned for growth" — no distinguishing facts

    ⚠ Test: cover the company name.  Could this sentence be
    about any large-cap tech stock?  If yes: score lower.

(3) QUANTIFICATION  ·  QT
    Are the key claims backed by numbers an investor could use?

    5 → Numbers throughout: multiples, growth rates, EPS
        impacts, time lags, loss rates, thresholds — the ones
        you'd need to actually model this position
    4 → Most claims quantified; one or two still qualitative
    3 → Several numbers present but central claims qualitative
    2 → One or two numbers; mostly qualitative
    1 → No numbers at all

    ⚠ Note: a short response can score 5 on QT if it packs
    the essential numbers.  A long response can score 2 if the
    numbers are decorative footnotes, not load-bearing claims.

(4) INVESTMENT USEFULNESS  ·  IU
    Does this response help an investor make a better decision?
    After reading it, do you know what to WATCH and what would
    CHANGE YOUR VIEW?

    5 → Gives you a specific threshold, trigger, or signal to
        monitor.  Actionable in the next portfolio review.
    4 → Mostly actionable; clear investment framework
    3 → Some useful content; partially actionable
    2 → Mild incremental value over what you already knew
    1 → Restates consensus; nothing new; no decision input

    ⚠ The key test: "If I read this before my investment
    committee meeting, does it change what I say?"

(5) COMPLETENESS  ·  CO
    Does the response address ALL parts of the question?
    Each question has 3 explicit sub-questions.  Count them.

    5 → Every sub-question answered with substance
    4 → All covered; one is thinner than the others
    3 → All mentioned; one is genuinely superficial
    2 → One sub-question clearly not addressed
    1 → Multiple sub-questions missing, or wrong company/topic

    ⚠ CO rewards coverage, not depth.  A 150-word response
    that answers all three parts of the question beats a
    300-word response that answers two of them beautifully.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SCORING EACH QUESTION  ·  The exact sequence
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

For EACH of the 10 questions, do this in order:

  1. Open questions/{QID}.txt
     Count the sub-questions.  Note what the perfect FIRST
     SENTENCE of an answer would look like.

  2. Read {QID}_A.txt  →  no scoring yet
     Read {QID}_B.txt  →  no scoring yet
     Read {QID}_C.txt  →  no scoring yet
     First impression only.  Who opened with the answer?
     Who gave you a number you could use?  Who felt vague?

  3. Score A on all five criteria.  Enter on score sheet.
     Score B on all five criteria.  Enter on score sheet.
     Score C on all five criteria.  Enter on score sheet.
     Score each response fully before moving to the next one.
     Do NOT score all three on DA, then all three on SP, etc.

  4. Sanity check (takes 60 seconds):
     · Re-read the first sentence of your highest-DA response.
       Does it actually open with the answer?
     · Re-count sub-questions for your highest-CO response.
       Does it actually address all of them?
     · Any score of 1 or 5?  Write a one-line note explaining it.

  5. Move to the next question.  Do not revise previous scores.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
PACE AND BREAKS
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Each question takes 15–20 minutes.  Do 5 questions, take a
30-minute break, then do the final 5.  Do not score all 10
in one sitting.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
WHEN YOU'RE DONE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Complete the TOTALS and FINAL SUMMARY sections of the score
sheet.  Submit to the coordinator.  Do not discuss your scores
with the other evaluator before the coordinator collects
both sheets.
