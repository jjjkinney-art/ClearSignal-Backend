The primary structural risk to services gross margin — and why the ranking matters
The regulatory pressure on App Store fees is the primary structural risk, but the framing requires precision. The App Store operates at approximately 47% gross margin within services overall — and the services segment itself runs at ~76% gross margin (Q1 FY2026: $30.0B in services revenue against $7.0B in services cost of sales, yielding 76.5%). The App Store is structurally the highest-margin line because Apple's platform infrastructure costs are largely fixed and don't scale linearly with commission revenue. sec
As of January 1, 2026, Apple replaced the flat EU commission structure with a layered 5% Core Technology Commission on digital goods revenue across all EU distribution paths, and the DOJ's 2024 antitrust suit is explicitly targeting App Store payment exclusivity in the US. The risk isn't just the current EU fee restructuring — it's that regulatory action in one jurisdiction creates legal precedent that accelerates action in others. A global commission cut from 30% toward 15-20% would reduce App Store gross revenue by $8-15 billion annually, but the margin impact is larger because the cost base doesn't compress proportionally. FunnelFox
The second risk is actually the more severe cliff in dollar terms, though it gets less attention: the Google default search deal, estimated at $18-20 billion annually in near-100% gross margin licensing revenue. This high-gross-profit licensing stream is part of Apple's services business, and its potential disruption via DOJ antitrust remedies targeting exclusive default search deals represents the single largest binary margin risk in Apple's services segment. If that revenue disappears without a substitute, the impact is approximately 14-16 percentage points of gross margin compression on services — falling from 76%+ to the low-60s. Apple Intelligence search features are the defensive hedge, but they are years away from generating comparable revenue. MacDailyNews
Advertising deceleration and iCloud pricing limits are tertiary — they are growth-rate headwinds, not margin-structure questions, and the market should price them very differently.
The attach rate saturation threshold
Services revenue per active device runs at approximately $44 per year based on FY2025 services revenue of ~$109 billion across the 2.5 billion device base. The math of services growth has two components: installed base growth (~6.4% in FY2025, adding 150 million devices) and per-device revenue growth (~7% in the same period), which sum to roughly 14% total services growth. Asymco
Saturation becomes a material headwind when installed base growth falls below approximately 4% AND per-device revenue growth decelerates below 4% simultaneously. At that combination — which produces total services growth below ~8% — the market can no longer support the growth-compounding narrative embedded in the current ~30-35× revenue multiple on services. The mechanism of saturation is geographic: as the installed base grows, the marginal new device is increasingly in an emerging or developing market where users subscribe to fewer premium services, creating a structural drag on average per-device monetization. High-income markets — the US, Western Europe, Japan, Australia — are approaching saturation in iCloud tiers, Apple Music subscriptions, and App Store spend per user. Every percentage point of installed base growth that comes from lower-ARPU geographies dilutes the per-device revenue average. Asymco
The earliest indicator and re-rating trigger
The right metric is implied services revenue per active device, computable quarterly from disclosed data. Apple reports both services revenue ($30.0B in Q1 FY2026) and installed base (2.5 billion active devices) every quarter. Dividing and annualizing gives a clean, real-time per-device monetization signal that precedes the headline services growth rate in revealing saturation, because headline growth can be masked for several quarters by pricing increases (subscription price hikes, improved Google deal terms) even as underlying attach rates plateau. sec
Current trajectory: per-device revenue grew approximately 7% YoY from Q1 FY2025 to Q1 FY2026 ($44.8 to $48.0 annualized). The watch zone begins at 3-5% per-device growth sustained for two quarters; below 3% for two consecutive quarters is the re-rating trigger. At that level, the compounding narrative breaks — services growth converges toward the installed base growth rate alone — and the appropriate multiple compresses from 30-35× revenue toward 18-22×, comparable to mature high-margin software businesses with stable but non-accelerating growth. On ~$120 billion in LTM services revenue, that multiple compression represents a very large reduction in the implied value of the services franchise embedded in Apple's consolidated equity price.
