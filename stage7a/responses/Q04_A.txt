Here's the full analytical framework.
Why capital market re-rating is the primary transmission channel
The instinct to frame this as an operational disruption story is understandable but wrong in a meaningful way. Operational disruption requires a scenario that is severe enough to impair production but recoverable enough to model — a blockade without physical destruction, or tensions that restrict logistics without kinetic engagement. In any scenario actually severe enough to structurally halt TSMC's Taiwan production, the stock reprices weeks or months before a single shipment is delayed.
TSM does not trade at Nvidia-like multiples for one simple reason: concentration in Taiwan and the associated political tail. The current valuation framework should be viewed as the value of advanced logic and AI cash flows minus a structural &quot;Taiwan discount&quot; in the multiple. That discount is present every single trading day regardless of the geopolitical temperature — it is already embedded in the price. A Strait escalation accelerates the magnitude of repricing, but the channel is re-rating, not operational disruption, because the financial damage is immediate and probabilistic rather than lagged and operational. Investing.com
The 2025 earthquake provides the cleaner operational disruption baseline: a certain number of wafers in process had to be scrapped, resulting in approximately NT$5.3 billion in earthquake losses net of insurance, pushing Q1 2025 guidance to the lower end of the range. That was a major physical event — magnitude 7.4 — and it produced a few basis points of margin impact recoverable within one quarter. A military escalation is categorically different in kind, not degree. sec
The discount — size and how it was derived
Two methodologies converge. The peer comparison approach is the more tractable. On almost every forward valuation metric, TSMC trades at a significant discount to ASML despite growing faster, generating expanding margins, and benefiting from the same AI-driven demand tailwind. TSMC's forward P/E of approximately 23× sits below ASML's ~37×. ASML is the nearest comparable — a structural monopoly in an enabling technology, domiciled in the Netherlands, with comparable moat characteristics. The 14-point P/E gap at roughly $15 in forward EPS translates to roughly $105-210/share of suppressed market price, or 20-30% of current market cap. TIKR
Most analysts put the geopolitical discount at 10-20% versus what TSM would trade at if headquartered in the US. The scenario-weighted DCF methodology produces a similar number: at a 7-10% annualized cross-strait conflict probability with near-zero residual value in that scenario, the expected value haircut is 15-25% applied to the geography-free DCF. What would most credibly reduce it
The Arizona fab narrative is structurally important but currently inadequate as a catalyst. TSMC's Fab 21 in Phoenix is estimated to produce up to 24,000 wafers per month, which accounts for only an estimated 2.3% of projected 2026 advanced wafer output. Construction of TSMC's second Arizona fab is complete, with production expected to begin in the second half of 2027. Even at full Arizona build-out, the discount does not compress materially before that capacity crosses ~15-20% of advanced revenue — which is a 2028-2030 story at the earliest.
