The coverage ratio and how it compares to pre-GFC
At Investor Day 2025, management confirmed the CRE portfolio remains consistent at ~$200 billion firmwide, predominantly multifamily in Classes B and C where they've seen limited losses. The Commercial Real Estate Banking segment within Commercial Banking alone runs ~$146 billion in loans as of Q1 2026. JPM doesn't separately disclose its office-specific coverage ratio, but management described building reserves to &quot;get ahead of the cycle&quot; beginning in 2023, and peer disclosures at peer banks put office-specific coverage in the 8-10% range. US Bancorp, for comparison, reserved &quot;aggressively&quot; for CRE office loans with a reserve ratio for overall CRE at 3.1% and office reserve coverage at 10%. J.P. Morgan Private Bank + 2
The pre-GFC comparison is instructive but not alarming in the way bears often suggest. Pre-GFC CRE coverage ran 0.5-1.0% on a book that was underwritten on the assumption of perpetual value appreciation. Current office coverage at ~8-10% was intentionally front-loaded. JPM's own analysis projects cumulative CMBS office sector losses of approximately 8.5% over 10 years — which means existing coverage roughly matches the long-run expected loss estimate, leaving little cushion if losses arrive faster than modeled but no gross underprovision by historical standards. The delinquency trigger level
CRE loan delinquencies reached 1.57% by Q4 2024 — the highest in a decade but well below crisis levels — with charge-offs remaining muted at 0.26% as banks continue to extend and restructure rather than foreclose. The 2.5% level is the first watch threshold. The 5-6% level is where the math becomes material to JPM's full-year EPS at scale. Yardi Kube
The more important signal to watch, however, is not the delinquency rate itself — it's the CECL macro scenario weights. JPM's Q1 2026 reserve model uses a weighted-average unemployment assumption of 5.6%, down from 5.8% the prior quarter. If that unemployment assumption moves above 6.5%, CRE and wholesale reserve builds follow mechanically under CECL before any delinquency data confirms the stress. That forward-looking provision mechanism means the income statement impact arrives 2-3 quarters ahead of the balance sheet damage — watch the unemployment assumption in the reserve model, not the published delinquency rate, as the true leading indicator.
