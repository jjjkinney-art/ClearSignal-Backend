The dollar impact calculation
The four hyperscalers — Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, and Meta — have collectively raised 2026 capex guidance to $725 billion, up 77% year-over-year from $410 billion in 2025. A –20 percentage point reduction in YoY growth implies the growth rate falls from +77% to roughly +57%, landing aggregate capex at ~$644 billion — a $81 billion reduction from current guidance. The single most important leading indicator
The hyperscaler capex-to-AI-cloud-revenue ratio is the one metric to own. AI-related services delivered roughly $25 billion in revenue in 2025 against more than $250 billion in infrastructure spending — about 10 cents of revenue per dollar of capex. That ratio needs to compress, not expand, for the capex cycle to sustain itself. It's reported every quarter across four separate earnings calls, making it the highest-frequency, most direct read on whether ROI is closing. BuildMVPFast
The current ratio sits around 9–10×. Watch for it flattening — and particularly for two consecutive quarters where AI cloud revenue growth falls below capex growth. That's the CFO's catalyst to pull back.
Where the bull thesis breaks
The structural break level is a capex/cloud-revenue ratio that rises above ~12× and sustains for two quarters. At that point, hyperscalers, whose capex already exceeds internal free cash flow and who are increasingly relying on debt markets to fund the buildout, face board-level pressure to enforce discipline. The analog is telco capex in 1999–2001 — the ratio peaked above 14× before a multi-year cutting cycle. Substack
The secondary risk compounding that thesis: Amazon has committed $25 billion additional to Anthropic alongside a 10-year commitment to AWS Trainium, with Trainium2 fully subscribed at 1.4 million chips deployed; Google announced up to $40 billion of investment in Anthropic along with 5 GW of dedicated TPU capacity. Custom silicon is no longer a roadmap story — it's a present-tense revenue item that directly shrinks NVIDIA's addressable share of the capex pool even without any top-line deceleration. Four customers accounted for 61% of NVIDIA's Q3 FY2026 revenue — a concentration that has grown from 36% to 61% in a single year.
