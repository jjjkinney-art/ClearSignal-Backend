Here's the full thesis, laid out precisely.
Why this isn't a merchandise-mix story
The conventional retail margin expansion playbook — sell more premium SKUs, improve category mix, reduce markdowns — has nothing to do with what Costco is doing. Costco's merchandise gross margin runs intentionally at around 11%, and management treats any drift upward as a signal to pass savings back to members rather than retain them. Gross margin increased only 20 basis points in FY2025, and even that was partly contaminated by gasoline price deflation on net sales. The expansion story lives entirely on a separate revenue line that functions like a SaaS subscription bolted onto a break-even retail operation. MarketScreener
Through the first 24 weeks of FY2026, Costco's merchandise operations produced $2.4 billion in operating income after SG&A, while membership fees contributed $2.68 billion — meaning the subscription layer now exceeds the entire retail operation in profit contribution. That's the structural point: Costco's gross margin expands not because it sells better products, but because a near-100%-margin fee stream grows faster than net sales. IndexBox
The flywheel mechanism
The mechanism is self-reinforcing. Membership income is high-margin, recurring, and predictable. The retail side operates on thin margins by design — those thin margins are what generate renewal rates above 90% globally and justify the fee. When the value proposition remains intact, Costco can raise fees periodically without churn. The September 2024 fee increase accounted for approximately 40% of membership income growth during FY2025, and the hike went through with no meaningful attrition. aolMarketScreener
The second layer of leverage is tier migration. Executive memberships now represent 75% of penetration of sales while paying double the base fee. Executive member sales penetration was approximately 73.6% of worldwide net sales in FY2025, meaning these higher-fee members are the franchise. As Gold Star members upgrade — driven by the 2% reward math closing easily for regular shoppers — the average revenue per member rises without any new recruitment cost. secSEC.gov
The best quarterly tracking metric
Organic membership fee income growth, stripped of the fee-hike effect and FX, is the cleanest signal. Excluding the fee hike and foreign exchange impacts, membership income grew 7.5% year over year in Q2 FY2026, driven by membership growth and executive membership upgrades. That ex-hike, ex-FX number strips away the noise and tells you whether the underlying subscription engine is still compounding. It leads reported gross margin by one to two quarters. Yahoo Finance
What causes reversal, and where the thesis breaks
The most active risk is renewal rate drift. Renewal rates slid 30 basis points in Q3 FY2025, then another 40 basis points in Q4, before moderating to 10 basis-point declines in each of Q1 and Q2 FY2026, with management signaling further modest pressure ahead. These are small absolute numbers, but the investment case depends on the market treating renewal rates as structurally perpetual, not as a meandering variable. CNBC
The valuation math is stark. Costco's current P/E of ~52× is 34% above its 10-year historical average of 39×. That premium reflects a market assigning near-zero probability to deceleration in the subscription engine. The break level is a global renewal rate sustained below 88% for two consecutive quarters alongside organic fee growth below 5%. At that level, the premise that Costco can raise fees every 7 years without churn collapses, and the multiple should re-rate toward the pre-flywheel historical average — 35-38× — which implies 25-30% downside from today's price without any deterioration in merchandise operations at all. Consecutive quarters of small declines signal a minor malfunction in an engine that once seemed built to never require maintenance. The irony is that the bull thesis doesn't need a crisis to break — it only needs the subscription machine to stop accelerating. TipRanks
