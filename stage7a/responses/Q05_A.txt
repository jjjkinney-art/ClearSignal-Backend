Has Meta fully recovered on a CPM-adjusted basis?
The honest answer is: in revenue terms, yes — and then some. In measurement-quality terms, not completely. Meta projected ATT would cost the business $10 billion in 2022 alone, and that materially landed: in Q1 2022 the average price per ad fell 8% year-over-year even as impressions grew 15% — the classic ATT signature of volume expanding while pricing collapsed under reduced advertiser confidence. The trough was deeper through full year 2022. IAPPsec
The recovery since then has been genuine. For full year 2025, average price per ad increased 9% year-over-year on top of 12% impression growth, and Q1 2026 showed price per ad up 12% on 19% impression growth. In absolute nominal terms, Meta's CPMs have compounded well past pre-ATT levels. sec
But the like-for-like quality-adjusted gap is real. Signal loss from iOS is estimated at 20-30%, which inflates apparent CPCs by hiding cheaper conversions — functioning as an &quot;invisible tax&quot; on attribution. Advertisers using the Conversions API (CAPI) with strong first-party data have largely bridged this gap individually, but the residual structural drag on iOS-origin impressions — which represent roughly half the US user base — keeps the effective measurement-quality CPM ~10-15% below a hypothetical no-ATT counterfactual. Meta's AI systems have generated enough incremental demand to offset this completely in revenue terms, but the underlying signal gap persists. Trendtrack
Reels vs. Feed monetization — the gap and timeline
Mark Zuckerberg described the dynamic clearly in 2023: &quot;The monetization efficiency of Reels is much less than Feed. So the more that Reels grows, even though it adds engagement to the system overall, it takes some time away from Feed and we actually lose money.&quot; That was the peak of the problem. CNBC
The gap has compressed substantially since. More than half of all ads on Meta's Instagram ran in Reels in 2025, up from 35% in 2024, and Instagram and Facebook Reels combined surpassed a $50 billion annual run rate in October 2025. On a CPM basis, Instagram Feed remains the highest-performing placement for conversion at 4.1x ROAS, while Reels offers efficient prospecting at 23% lower CPM. That gap was 40%+ in 2022; it's now roughly 23-26%. CNBCMHI Media
Full parity is structurally unlikely because the format-level user intent difference is real — Feed scroll is higher-intent than passive Reels consumption. The realistic endpoint is a 10-15% persistent Reels discount, reachable by 2027-2028 as shoppable Reels and commerce formats scale. The volume offset has already resolved the revenue drag concern: while individual Reels ads still generate less revenue than feed ads, the sheer volume of time people spend watching them is starting to overcome that efficiency gap. The single metric that confirms permanent ATT normalization
The definitive confirmation signal is average price per ad growing faster than ad impressions for four or more consecutive quarters. When that ratio inverts — price leading volume, not lagging it — it proves demand is outpacing supply, which only happens when advertiser ROI confidence is strong enough to sustain competitive bid escalation. In Q1 2026, ad impressions grew 19% and average price per ad grew 12% — volume is still running ahead of price, meaning inventory growth is partially outpacing advertiser confidence recovery. When that flips sustainably, it closes the analytical case. Quantumrun
The secondary signal: Advantage+ Shopping Campaigns now represent 62% of ecommerce conversion spend, up from 34% in 2024, delivering 17% lower CPA than manual campaigns. Sustained declining reported CPA within Advantage+ alongside rising CPMs would confirm the AI stack has definitively reproduced the targeting efficiency that ATT removed — not by restoring deterministic tracking, but by building a probabilistic AI system that outperforms it.
