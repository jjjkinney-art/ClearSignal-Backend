Structural vs. cyclical — the honest answer is both, and the proportion matters
The expansion from the 2023 trough (~24%) to the 2025 peak (39.5%) had four distinct components that need to be separated:
The two genuinely structural drivers are custom silicon economics and managed services mix. Amazon CEO Andy Jassy stated on the Q1 2026 call that Trainium chips now power more than 50% of Amazon Bedrock token usage, and that custom silicon could save Amazon &quot;tens of billions of capex dollars per year&quot; and provide &quot;several hundred basis points of operating margin advantage&quot; versus relying on external chips for inference workloads. This mechanism permanently lowers the cost of revenue for AI compute — it isn't a cost-cycle artifact, it's vertical integration eliminating Nvidia margin from Amazon's P&L. The Trainium business crossed a $20 billion annual revenue run rate with $225 billion in forward revenue commitments, giving it a demand runway that underpins the structural case. BenzingaAlphaSense
The two cyclical drivers are the enterprise workload reacceleration and the depreciation schedule extension. The 2023 trough was caused by a synchronized enterprise optimization cycle that temporarily depressed consumption against a fixed cost base; the subsequent reacceleration produced classic operating leverage that looked like margin expansion but was really just volume recovery. The server useful-life extension from 5 to 6 years added several hundred basis points mechanically in the implementation period without any operational improvement.
The current margin and where the ceiling is
AWS operating margin fell to 37.7% in Q1 2026 from 39.1% in Q4 2025, a 140-basis-point decline reflecting early AI infrastructure depreciation. That compression is the visible arrival of the D&A wave from the capex ramp. Amazon's D&A on property and equipment reached $41.9 billion in FY2025, up 30.5% year-over-year, and cumulative capex through 2027 is now tracking at $344 billion, meaning annual D&A will grow by another $15-20 billion within 18-24 months. The structural ceiling is approximately 38-40%. The Trainium advantage and mix shift can sustain the 35-38% range durably. Breaking above 40% and staying there requires either Trainium3/4 delivering another step-change in inference cost reduction, a managed services pricing reset that separates Bedrock/SageMaker pricing from commodity compute rates, or demand monetization on the $200B capex arriving faster than the depreciation ramp. CFO Olsavsky explicitly framed the margin dynamic as a function of the investment cycle: &quot;We expect AWS operating margins to fluctuate over time, driven in part by the level of investments we are making at any point in time.&quot; Data Center Dynamics
The single metric that signals peak
AWS operating income in Q1 2026 was $14.2 billion on $37.6 billion in revenue, with operating margin at 37.7% — down from 39.1% sequentially, and OI growth (+23%) running below revenue growth (+28%). That negative spread between OI growth and revenue growth for two consecutive quarters is the definitive peak signal, because it means depreciation is growing faster than revenue's ability to absorb it. Revenue growth rate alone is the wrong metric — AWS can grow revenue 28% while the expansion has already peaked if D&A is growing at 30%. Margin percentage is noisy due to quarterly capacity timing. AWS operating income dollars in absolute terms, compared to the revenue growth rate in the same quarter, is the cleanest read: when OI dollars grow meaningfully slower than revenue for two consecutive quarters, the expansion cycle has turned.
